## Introduction to Salesforce

### 1. First Understand One Important Thing

Salesforce is NOT just one software.

Salesforce is a huge cloud-based business platform.

It mainly started as a CRM company.

But over time it expanded into:

- sales
- customer service
- marketing
- analytics
- AI
- automation
- app development
- e-commerce
- integrations
- collaboration
- industry solutions

Today Salesforce is a massive ecosystem.

---

### 2. Before Salesforce Existed

Businesses faced many problems.

Customer information was scattered everywhere.

Example:

- Excel sheets
- notebooks
- emails
- employee memory
- physical files

Businesses struggled with:

- managing customers
- tracking sales
- customer support
- reporting
- scaling operations

Traditional software also had problems.

Companies had to:

- buy servers
- install software manually
- maintain infrastructure
- update systems themselves

This was expensive and difficult.

---

### 3. Salesforce Was Created To Solve These Problems

Salesforce introduced a new idea.

Instead of:

"Install software on your own machines"

Salesforce said:

"Use software directly from the internet."

This became known as:

Cloud Computing

More specifically:

SaaS

Software as a Service

---

### 4. Very Simple Meaning of Salesforce

Salesforce is a cloud platform that helps businesses:

- manage customers
- track sales
- support customers
- automate processes
- analyze data
- build applications
- integrate systems

All from one connected platform.

---

### 5. What Does Salesforce Aim To Achieve?

Salesforce mainly aims to help businesses:

- build better customer relationships
- improve efficiency
- automate business operations
- increase sales
- improve customer satisfaction
- centralize business data
- make better decisions using analytics

Main philosophy:

"Customer should be at the center of business."

---

### 6. Real World Example — Without Salesforce

Imagine a company selling laptops.

Different departments work separately.

Sales team uses Excel.

Support team uses emails.

Marketing team uses another tool.

Problems happen:

- duplicate customer data
- missed follow-ups
- poor communication
- slow support
- bad reporting

Everything becomes disconnected.

---

### 7. Real World Example — With Salesforce

Now same company uses Salesforce.

Everything becomes centralized.

Sales team:

- tracks leads
- manages opportunities
- follows pipeline

Support team:

- handles customer cases
- sees customer history

Marketing team:

- sends campaigns
- tracks engagement

Management:

- sees dashboards
- tracks revenue
- monitors performance

Everything is connected.
