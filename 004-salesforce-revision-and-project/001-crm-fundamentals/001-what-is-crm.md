## What CRM Means

Imagine you own a small shop.

At the beginning, you have only a few customers.

You remember everything manually:

- Who bought what
- Who still has pending payments
- Who likes which products
- Who called yesterday
- Who needs support

This works when you have 5 customers.

But what happens when you have:

- 500 customers?
- 5 sales employees?
- 3 support agents?
- 2 marketing teams?
- Customers from different countries?

Now things become messy.

You forget customer details.
Customers repeat the same issue multiple times.
Salespeople lose leads.
Support teams do not know previous conversations.
Marketing sends irrelevant emails.

This is the problem CRM was created to solve.

---

### 1. Full Form of CRM

CRM stands for:

Customer Relationship Management

Let us understand this word slowly.

#### Customer

The people or companies who buy your product or service.

Examples:

- Amazon customers
- Netflix subscribers
- Bank account holders
- Students using an online course platform

---

#### Relationship

A business does not just want one-time customers.

It wants long-term relationships.

For example:

A bank wants:
- You to open an account
- Then take a credit card
- Then take a loan
- Then invest money

So the bank continuously maintains a relationship with you.

---

#### Management

Managing means:

- Storing information
- Tracking activities
- Organizing processes
- Automating work
- Helping teams collaborate

---

So overall:

CRM means managing a company's relationship with customers using processes and software.

---

### 2. Why CRM Became Necessary

Before CRM systems existed, companies used:

- Excel sheets
- Papers
- Emails
- Sticky notes
- Phone books

This caused many problems.

---

#### Example Without CRM

Suppose a customer named Rahul calls a mobile company.

Day 1:
- Rahul complains about network issue.
- Support agent writes it in a notebook.

Day 3:
- Rahul calls again.
- Another agent answers.
- That agent has no idea about previous complaint.

Rahul becomes frustrated because he must explain everything again.

Now imagine this happening millions of times.

The company loses customers.

---

### 3. Real World Analogy of CRM

Think of CRM as a "digital brain" of a company.

Just like your brain remembers:

- Friends

CRM remembers customer information for the business.

A CRM can remember:

- Customer name
- Phone number
- Email
- Purchase history
- Complaints
- Meetings
- Payments
- Support tickets
- Interests
- Follow-ups

Everything is stored in one centralized system.

### 4. Simple Real World Example — Medical Clinic

When a patient arrives:

The CRM stores:
- Patient name

- Phone number

- Medicines

Now when the patient visits again:

- Previous treatment

### 11. Types of CRM

There are mainly 3 types of CRM.

---

### 12. Operational CRM

Focuses on automating daily business operations.

Examples:
- Sales automation
- Email automation
- Ticket management

#### Real World Example

When you submit a support form on a website:
- Ticket automatically gets created.
- Support team automatically gets notified.

That automation is operational CRM.

---

### 13. Analytical CRM

Focuses on analyzing customer data.

Businesses study:
- Customer behavior
- Buying trends
- Revenue patterns

#### Example

Netflix analyzes:
- Which shows users watch
- Which genres are popular

Then recommends personalized content.

That is analytical CRM behavior.

---

### 14. Collaborative CRM

Focuses on sharing customer information across teams.

Example:

Sales team and support team both access same customer data.

So:
- Everyone stays informed.
- Customer does not repeat information again and again.

### 24. Cloud CRM vs On-Premise CRM

CRM systems are commonly deployed in two ways.

#### Cloud CRM

Software runs on the internet.

Examples:
- Salesforce
- HubSpot CRM

Advantages:
- Accessible from anywhere
- Lower setup cost
- Automatic updates

---

#### On-Premise CRM

Software is installed on company servers.

Advantages:
- More control over data
- Better for strict security requirements

Disadvantages:
- Higher maintenance cost
- Requires IT infrastructure
